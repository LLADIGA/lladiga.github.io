LLADIGA investigates whether artificial agents can learn language in this purposeful, sample-efficient, and ecologically sustainable way.

Project Motivation

From Passive Text Prediction to Purposeful Communication

Bridging cognitive inspirations from child language acquisition with principled multi-turn learning and evaluation for language models.

Large Language Models (LLMs) have learned language through incremental, data-intensive phases: first by learning to predict the next word in massive text corpora, next by learning to follow instructions, and finally by aligning outputs to human preferences. This is Artificial Intelligence nowadays—powerful, yet extraordinarily data-hungry.

Human children are dramatically more sample-efficient: they need vastly less linguistic input to become fluent communicators. Why? Because children do not passively absorb static archives of text; they learn to use language for a purpose—to coordinate, ask questions, resolve misunderstandings, and interact with their caregivers and the physical and social world they inhabit.

LLADIGA (Learning Language with Dialogue Games) addresses this foundational question directly. By turning structured, goal-oriented, multi-turn dialogue games into active learning arenas, we investigate how language models can acquire communicative and reasoning capabilities from interaction signals rather than sheer corpus scale—making AI training not only more transparent and grounded, but also substantially less data-hungry and less ecologically damaging.

LLADIGA is a 3-year bilateral project carried out jointly by the University of Potsdam (led by Prof. Dr. David Schlangen) and the Free University of Bozen-Bolzano (unibz) (led by Prof. Raffaella Bernardi) within the bilateral collaboration framework between the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG) — Priority Programme SPP 2556 “LaSTing” — and the Autonomous Province of Bolzano – South Tyrol (Joint Projects D-SUD LAV).

🎯 Language as Goal-Directed Action

In dialogue games, language simultaneously defines the task constraints (rules) and serves as the medium of action—requiring perspective-taking, pragmatic adaptation, and multi-turn planning.

🌿 Sample & Ecological Efficiency

Replacing brute-force corpus scaling with rich interactive feedback targets stronger generalization in compact, open-weights language models with a fraction of the energy footprint.

🔬 Behavioral & Internal Probing

Controlled game environments disentangle rule-following from strategic competence externally while providing clean diagnostic lenses into internal model representations and parameter updates.
